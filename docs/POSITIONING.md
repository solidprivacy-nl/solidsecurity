# Positioning Doctrine

## Primary customer-facing position

**Aantoonbaar in control. Zonder eigen complianceafdeling.**

SolidSecurity manages the recurring work required to demonstrate structured security, privacy and responsible AI governance for small organizations.

## Secondary promise

**Eén keer organiseren. Meervoudig aantonen. Continu onderhouden.**

The same controls and evidence are reused across relevant regulatory, sector and assurance contexts.

## AI messaging rule

Do not lead with “AI compliance”. Lead with the business outcome and trust model.

Preferred sequence:

1. customer outcome — demonstrably in control;
2. scope — security, privacy and AI governance;
3. service model — managed, recurring, no internal department required;
4. credibility — evidence and professional review;
5. mechanism — AI/automation removes repetitive work and therefore improves speed and economics.

## Primary Care ICP — launch hypothesis

The primary R2 launch hypothesis is a Dutch healthcare organization that:

- has material security/privacy/compliance obligations and recurring evidence requests;
- lacks a proportionate internal security/compliance function able to maintain the work continuously;
- has enough operational complexity that spreadsheets, one-off consultancy and ad-hoc policy work create recurring friction;
- has a credible budget/ability to buy a managed professional service if the value hypothesis is confirmed;
- can nominate an accountable internal sponsor and provide the minimum facts/evidence needed for a managed workflow;
- values an evidence-backed managed outcome rather than another self-service GRC tool.

`50–250 FTE` is a **commercial segmentation hypothesis only**. It is not a legal threshold, applicability rule or automatic qualification condition.

### Explicit disqualifiers / poor fit

Do not force-fit organizations that:

- want an automatic certification/compliance guarantee or AI-only final verdict;
- require SolidSecurity to act as certification body, legal counsel or unrestricted CISO replacement by default;
- refuse the minimum accountable customer participation or evidence access needed for a credible assessment;
- primarily need SOC/MDR, penetration testing, generic vulnerability management or another technical operations service rather than managed assurance/compliance;
- are so complex that the bounded Care offer cannot credibly cover the required scope without a different service design;
- are too early/low-pressure for recurring managed assurance to have demonstrable value.

Supplier remains a secondary reuse/expansion track until E3 market evidence justifies promotion.

## Regulatory positioning matrix

Customer-facing urgency follows proven applicability, never generic regulatory fear.

| Situation | Permitted positioning | Not permitted without further proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct Cyberbeveiligingswet/NIS2 applicability explicitly established | Direct Cbw/NIS2 readiness and evidence-maintenance positioning within the proven scope | Claims beyond the assessed entity/scope or legal conclusion not established by the applicability decision |
| Direct Cbw/NIS2 applicability not established | NEN 7510/Wabvpz, privacy, IGJ/chain/customer assurance pressure, cyber resilience and responsible-AI governance where actually relevant | “You are subject to NIS2/Cbw” or equivalent urgency claim |
| Audit/certification ambition | Audit/readiness preparation and evidence-system maintenance | Claiming certification, auditor independence or guaranteed certification outcome |
| Supplier/customer questionnaire pressure | Evidence/assertion reuse and maintained supplier-assurance dossier | Claiming external assurance or certification solely from internal review |

Applicability remains a governed decision with source, scope, rationale, uncertainty and human review provenance.

## Credibility claims

Safe claims:

- “AI and automation reduce repetitive compliance work.”
- “Material conclusions are professionally reviewed.”
- “One control can support multiple frameworks.”
- “We maintain evidence and actions continuously rather than producing only a one-off report.”
- “We prepare organizations for independent audits; certification remains with the certification body.”

Claims to avoid:

- “100% compliant.”
- “AI guarantees NIS2/ISO/NEN compliance.”
- “Automatic certification.”
- “Replace your DPO/CISO/auditor completely.”
- “Cheapest in the market.”

## Alternative / incumbent MSP response

The launch comparison is not “SolidSecurity versus nobody”. Customers may already use an MSP/IT supplier, consultant, GRC SaaS, accountant/auditor or internal spreadsheet process.

Core response to **“our MSP/IT supplier already handles this”**:

- keep the MSP for infrastructure/technical operations where it is strong;
- test whether it actually owns the cross-cutting requirement→control→implementation→evidence→professional-review lifecycle;
- SolidSecurity is complementary where the missing capability is governed evidence, applicability, recurring assurance workflow and reusable management proof across technical and non-technical controls;
- if the incumbent already performs that complete managed capability credibly and economically, the prospect is a poor fit rather than a forced sale.

This is a testable positioning hypothesis, not a claim that MSPs categorically cannot provide compliance services.

## “Better, faster, cheaper” interpretation

Do not use this as an unsupported headline. Prove it operationally:

- **better** = consistent control model, traceability, evidence state and review;
- **faster** = reuse and AI-assisted production;
- **cheaper** = fewer professional hours spent on repetitive administration;
- **credible** = human accountability remains at material decision points.
